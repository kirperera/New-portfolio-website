## Summary

Urban mobility and equitable access to essential services are fundamental to public health. This spatial analysis project examined **180 metropolitan census tracts** to measure accessibility to secondary and tertiary medical facilities under a strict **15-minute travel threshold** (combining pedestrian walking and scheduled public bus lines).

Using spatial graph algorithms in **OSMnx** and vector geometries in **PostGIS**, the study revealed that **42,300 residents (18.6% of the municipal population)** resided in acute transit deserts. By simulating the addition of three strategic transit stops, spatial coverage within the 15-minute envelope increased from **81.4% to 94.2%**.

## Method

1. **Topological Graph Construction:** Downloaded and cleaned walkable multi-digraph networks via Python's `OSMnx` library, imputing pedestrian walking speeds (calibrated to 4.5 km/h) with elevation penalties.
2. **Isochrone Generation:** Calculated 5-, 10-, and 15-minute concave hull polygons around each medical facility using Dijkstra's shortest path algorithm over the dual transit-pedestrian graph.
3. **Spatial Aggregation in PostGIS:** Stored geometries in **PostGIS (SRID 3857)**; executed spatial intersections (`ST_Intersects`, `ST_Area`, `ST_Difference`) to compute the exact population residing outside the 15-minute envelope.
4. **Interactive Map Engineering:** Exported optimized GeoJSON vector tiles into an interactive **MapLibre GL JS** web interface with dynamic layer toggling and choropleth filtering.

```sql
-- Calculating census tract population outside 15-minute healthcare isochrones
WITH merged_isochrones AS (
  SELECT ST_Union(geom) AS safe_zone_geom 
  FROM spatial_healthcare_isochrones 
  WHERE travel_time_mins <= 15
),
tract_coverage AS (
  SELECT 
    c.tract_id,
    c.total_population,
    c.geom AS tract_geom,
    COALESCE(
      ST_Area(ST_Intersection(c.geom, m.safe_zone_geom)) / NULLIF(ST_Area(c.geom), 0), 
      0
    ) AS coverage_ratio
  FROM dim_census_tracts c
  CROSS JOIN merged_isochrones m
)
SELECT 
  tract_id,
  total_population,
  ROUND((coverage_ratio * 100)::numeric, 1) AS pct_covered,
  ROUND((total_population * (1 - coverage_ratio))::numeric) AS underserved_residents
FROM tract_coverage
WHERE coverage_ratio < 0.50
ORDER BY underserved_residents DESC;
```

## Results & Spatial Insights

- **The Peripheral Penalty:** While 96% of downtown inhabitants had access to care within 8 minutes, peripheral northern and southeastern tracts averaged **38 minutes of transit time** to the nearest clinic.
- **Vulnerable Demographics:** Underserved zones had a **24% higher proportion of elderly citizens (65+)** compared to well-connected central neighborhoods.
- **Route Optimization:** Adding 3 targeted feeder bus connections at key highway overpasses reduced the population living in transit deserts by **68%** with zero new capital expenditure on vehicles.

---

## Limitations and Ethics

- **Time-of-Day Variations:** The accessibility analysis utilized weekday midday transit headways; late-night emergency accessibility is significantly worse due to reduced bus frequencies.
- **Physical Barrier Assumptions:** Network analysis relied on mapped pedestrian crossings; unmapped construction detours and missing sidewalk ramps were not systematically modeled.

---

## Recommendations & Next Steps

1. **Implement On-Demand Micro-Transit:** Deploy on-demand shuttle services for northern census tracts rather than fixed 40-foot bus routes to maximize cost efficiency.
2. **Ambulance Staging Allocation:** Relocate two emergency vehicle staging sites to the perimeter of the identified coverage dead zones.
3. **Future Extension:** Integrate real-time GTFS-RT telemetry to build a live accessibility monitoring dashboard for city officials.
