## Summary

Flight delays cost the commercial aviation sector over **$30 billion annually** in passenger compensation, wasted crew hours, and inefficient gate utilization. This project created a machine learning classification engine that predicts whether a scheduled flight will experience a departure delay exceeding 15 minutes (the FAA standard threshold).

By fusing national flight schedule records with real-time **NOAA meteorological METAR observations** and historical turnaround buffers across 450,000 domestic US flights, the resulting gradient-boosted pipeline achieved an **ROC-AUC of 0.834** and an **F1-score of 0.74** on unseen test data.

## Context and Objective

- **Role:** ML Engineer & Data Scientist (Personal Project)
- **Objective:** Provide airport operations and ground handling teams with actionable delay risk scores 2 hours prior to pushback to optimize baggage transfer and ramp logistics.
- **Scope:** 2.4 million flight records across the top 30 US hub airports across seasonal cycles.

## Method

1. **Feature Engineering & Leakage Prevention:** Built temporal lag features for inbound tail numbers (`tail_number_delay_lag1`) ensuring strict separation between pre-departure signals and post-gate departure events to prevent data leakage.
2. **Handling Class Imbalance:** Departure delays (>15 min) comprised approximately **19.4%** of flights. Evaluated scale-pos-weight adjustments and focal loss rather than synthetic oversampling to preserve calibrated probability outputs.
3. **Model Selection & Hyperparameter Tuning:** Benchmarked Logistic Regression, Random Forest, LightGBM, and XGBoost using 5-fold rolling time-series cross-validation. Tuned hyper-parameters (learning rate, tree depth, subsample ratio) via **Optuna** across 100 trials.
4. **Model Explainability:** Evaluated global and local feature attribution using **SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)** TreeExplainer.

```python
import xgboost as xgb
from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score, average_precision_score

# Tuned XGBoost Classifier with calibrated class weighting
model = xgb.XGBClassifier(
    n_estimators=650,
    max_depth=6,
    learning_rate=0.035,
    subsample=0.85,
    colsample_bytree=0.8,
    scale_pos_weight=4.15,
    tree_method="hist",
    random_state=42
)

model.fit(X_train, y_train, eval_set=[(X_val, y_val)], verbose=False)
y_pred_proba = model.predict_proba(X_test)[:, 1]

roc_score = roc_auc_score(y_test, y_pred_proba)
pr_score = average_precision_score(y_test, y_pred_proba)
print(f"Test ROC-AUC: {roc_score:.4f} | PR-AUC: {pr_score:.4f}")
```

## Results & Model Evaluation

- **Performance Metrics:** The model reached **83.4% ROC-AUC** and **76.2% Precision-Recall AUC**, outperforming the existing historical average baseline (61.2% ROC-AUC) by **22.2 percentage points**.
- **SHAP Key Insights:** The three most influential predictors were:
  1. *Inbound aircraft arrival delay* (contributing 34% of predictive weight)
  2. *Origin airport departure runway queue depth* (22% weight)
  3. *Crosswind component exceeding 20 knots at departure runway* (14% weight)
- **Calibration:** Brier score was **0.112**, indicating well-calibrated probabilistic output suitable for operational risk thresholds.

---

## Limitations and Ethics

- **Air Traffic Control (ATC) Tactical Constraints:** Real-time ground stop orders and flow control changes issued by the FAA are published dynamically and could not be scraped ahead of the 2-hour prediction horizon.
- **Regional Bias:** The training data was focused on top-30 primary hub airports; generalization to secondary regional aerodromes requires additional domain adaptation.
